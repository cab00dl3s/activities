Race Assessment

STRENGTHS

▲ Aerobic base is robust. 415 km run + 376 km hiked in the past 12 months. Consistent multi-sport load means your cardiovascular system handles long duration well.
▲ Mountain legs from July 2025. 11 days of alpine hiking with up to 1,799m D+ per day. Your legs know what sustained steep ascent feels like.
▲ Excellent flat run speed. Avg 6:27/km on flat 16km runs. You have headroom to lose pace on climbs and still bank time.
▲ Controlled HR under load. Most long efforts clock HR 110–155 — aerobic zone, not redlining. Race-day cardiac ceiling is not the limiter.
▲ Rock climbing proprioception. Technical terrain confidence translates directly to rocky Dolomite trails.

RISKS & WATCH POINTS

▼ Low run-specific vertical. Only 452m total D+ in May runs. Trail legs ≠ flat legs when you hit 1,115m of real climbing in race mode.
▼ Longest trail run is 12.6 km. The race is 20 km. The final 7+ km at altitude will be uncharted fatigue territory — pace conservatively in the first half.
▼ Altitude at Cortina (1,224m+). With peaks at ~2,000m, expect 5–8% reduced oxygen delivery. If not altitude-acclimatised, add buffer to your pace.
▼ 5pm start = evening racing. Temperature drop after sunset. Carry mandatory 500ml fluid but plan for cooling conditions on the descent.
▼ No poles allowed. Pure leg strength on the descents. Quad conditioning is critical; the downhill after km 15 will test you.
